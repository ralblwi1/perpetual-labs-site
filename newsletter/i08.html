Aggregate benchmarks mislead about where data is needed — distribution-matched eval is required

Two debates collide productively here: whether emergent abilities are real or a metric artifact, and whether pretraining loss even tracks downstream performance. The mirage result shows benchmark jumps can be measurement choices; the downstream-scaling result shows more pretraining can improve loss while worsening the actual task under distribution mismatch. The shared lesson — that headline benchmarks are an unreliable guide to where capability (and therefore data) is lacking — implies you must measure on the deployment distribution, which makes bespoke, task-matched evaluation a prerequisite, not a nicety.

// evidence — extracted from the papers
[01]Apparent capability jumps can be artifacts of the metric, not the model.
“Nonlinear or discontinuous metrics produce apparent emergent abilities; linear metrics yield smooth, predictable changes.”
▸ Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage? — Rylan Schaeffer et al., 2023 (arXiv:2304.15004)
[02]Some abilities genuinely appear only past scale thresholds — emergence is contested.
“An ability is 'emergent' if not present in smaller models but present in larger models, appearing as sharp, unpredictable transitions.”
▸ Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models — Jason Wei et al., 2022 (arXiv:2206.07682)
[03]Pretraining loss can improve while the downstream task gets worse.
“Under moderate misalignment, BLEU can fluctuate or worsen with more pretraining even as downstream cross-entropy monotonically improves.”
▸ Scaling Laws for Downstream Task Performance of Large Language Models — Berivan Isik et al., 2024 (arXiv:2402.04177)
[04]Performance lives in a low-dimensional capability space — aggregates hide structure.
“LM performance is a function of a low-dimensional capability space; model families differ mainly in compute-to-capability efficiency.”
▸ Observational Scaling Laws and the Predictability of Language Model Performance — Yangjun Ruan et al., 2024 (arXiv:2405.10938)

why it's non-obvious

The emergence debate and the loss-vs-downstream debate are usually discussed separately. Joining them to conclude that aggregate benchmarks cannot tell you where to invest data — only distribution-matched evals can — is the synthesis.

implication for perpetual labs

Buyers cannot trust MMLU-style aggregates to find their capability gaps, so deployment-distribution-matched evaluation is a prerequisite for knowing where to invest data at all — the measurement problem precedes the data-creation decision, and bespoke task-aligned eval is the way to locate the real gap.

limitations — what this does not show

The emergence debate is not settled in the mirage camp's favor. Wei et al. [2] document cases — e.g. 14 BIG-Bench tasks where PaLM 62B achieves above-random performance while LaMDA 137B and GPT-3 175B are at chance — that are not obviously metric artifacts.
